### Editorial principle

The aspiration is **the clarity of an excellent research paper explained to a curious non-specialist**, not academic jargon, clever slogans or inflated confidence.

Every paragraph must answer one reader question and perform one of these functions:
1. **Context** — What was the real situation and whose problem was it?
2. **Gap** — Why was the existing approach insufficient? What evidence supports that judgment?
3. **Question** — What plausible uncertainty or hypothesis followed?
4. **Method / comparison** — What was checked, what options were actually considered, and what could be implemented?
5. **Decision + cost** — What was chosen, why, and what did it sacrifice?
6. **Observation** — What actually shipped or changed; what was measured versus merely inferred?
7. **Contribution + limitation** — What design judgment can be learned from the case, and what would be tested next?

**No unsupported causal claims.** Keep observed facts, interpretations and untested hypotheses distinguishable without repeating the same disclaimer in every section. Comparison of a *hypothetical* alternative with a chosen direction is not evidence that a historical alternative prototype existed. Do not fabricate research, interview participants, product-market fit, operational lift or technical implementation.

### CTR: required bridge between iterations

**Observed (requires analytics source check):** After first preview-carousel implementation, about 5% CTR was recorded. The user reports that about 20% moved the carousel at least once. The two rates may have different denominators and are not a verified conversion funnel.

**Interpretation, not established fact:** Some people explored the carousel without opening a detail page; it is plausible that content relevance was hard to recognise quickly.

**Hypothesis:** If the types and value of brand updates were legible before opening a detail page, more relevant discovery could occur.

**Method:** Switch from what the *supplier wants to announce* to what the *gifticon customer might want to find*. Study popular customer communities and reclassify the full existing events/content inventory based on findings; try to make the taxonomy mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive, documenting overlaps and exceptions.

**Decision:** Category labels/chips in the preview cards, constrained by actual crawled data and feasible management. Verify if manual categorisation was used, who handled it, and its ongoing costs before asserting implementation details.

**Observed result:** ~10% CTR after second iteration in the old project record; do not equate observational change to causal experiment or downstream purchase growth.

### Editing pass / acceptance tests

- Each chapter title should make a specific assertion, not announce a generic UX stage.
- Each paragraph should have exactly one job: if its content is elsewhere, cut or merge.
- The reader should understand **why the next decision follows** without guessing or needing UX expertise.
- A decision's alternatives and costs must be grounded; label analytic comparisons as analytical.
- Use technical language only when it clarifies: explain **CTR, MECE, PoC, Lottie and access control** in plain context on first use.
- Keep results prominent and honest. Put non-critical measurement caveats and source gaps once in the appropriate **Evidence & Limitations** area.
- Keep original screenshots and `?versions=1` edit/image request annotations until sources are supplied. Do not alter historical materials to manufacture proof.
- Compare desktop/mobile, Korean/English, light/dark, keyboard and reduced-motion behavior before merge.
- Preserve v1.4 on `main`; prepare v1.5 as an independent reviewable Draft PR.
